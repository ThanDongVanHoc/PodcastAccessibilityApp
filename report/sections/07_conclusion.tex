\section{Conclusion}

Accessibility in Compose is not a checkbox but control over the semantics tree at every level: naming
and hiding nodes correctly (§\ref{sec:core}), then grouping, ordering, and consolidating their
actions (§\ref{sec:adv}). The payoff is measurable --- a podcast card drops from six focus stops to
one, the resume banner is announced first, and playback state is spoken the instant it changes --- so
a TalkBack user completes the full Discover~$\rightarrow$~Detail~$\rightarrow$~Player flow without
sight.

Verification matters as much as construction (§\ref{sec:test}): TalkBack judges the listening
experience, Scanner and ATF triage mechanical defects, Layout Inspector explains structure, and
Compose tests lock semantics against regressions. No layer suffices alone, and --- as the Agoda
capture showed --- a tool's raw suggestion is a hypothesis, not a defect.

The practical lesson: build accessibility in from the first composable. Prefer Material components for
their default semantics, add custom semantics only where the design forces it, and test with the
assistive technology users actually run.

\subsection*{Future work}

Version~2: run TalkBack on our own build across a device/version matrix, capture Layout Inspector
evidence for representative findings, wire ATF into CI, and --- resources permitting --- have users
who rely on assistive technology evaluate the core tasks.
