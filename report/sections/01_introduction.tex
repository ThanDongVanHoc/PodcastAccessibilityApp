\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Why accessibility}

About one in six people lives with a disability that changes how they use a phone or a computer: for a vision-impaired user
the screen reader \emph{is} the interface, for a hearing-impaired user the subtitles are how they ``hear". Accessibility is the practice of building software so
these users get equal access. Since June~2022 (EAA, §\ref{sec:legal}) it is a legal
requirement in the European Union (EU)

We chose a podcast/audiobook player on purpose: audio is one of the few digital experiences that user can consume regardless of their vision if the surrounding controls
are reachable. The entire accessibility gap here lives in the UI, which makes it a clean case study.

\subsection{Goal and scope}

We took a conventional, accessibility-blind podcast UI and rebuilt it to be fully operable by
TalkBack, Switch Access, and 200\% fonts, justifying every technique. The deliverable is a
front-end only Jetpack Compose app, where data is mocked to replicate working backed, plus
this report, in four parts:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
	\item \textbf{Background} (§\ref{sec:bg}) --- the standards and the Android accessibility model.
	\item \textbf{Core semantics} (§\ref{sec:core}) --- labels, roles, state, touch targets, fonts.
	\item \textbf{Advanced semantics} (§\ref{sec:adv}) --- merging, traversal order, custom actions.
	\item \textbf{Testing \& evaluation} (§\ref{sec:test}) --- verification and honest trade-offs.
\end{itemize}
